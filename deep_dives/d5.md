## Prioritization & tradeoffs

Ask five people in most organizations why the backlog is ordered the way it is, and you'll typically get five different, half-remembered answers — the loudest stakeholder, an executive's pet request, whatever shipped fastest last time. The backlog reflects whoever argued most recently, not a deliberate investment logic anyone could reconstruct without asking the PM directly.

This dimension measures whether prioritization is a negotiation that happens to a backlog, or a governed, evidence-backed decision process the backlog is a byproduct of.

### Where most organizations start (Nascent)

Prioritization is an informal, recurring negotiation — no consistent framework, no documented tradeoff rationale, no stable authority over who actually decides. The first real step is naming a consistent method and requiring documented rationale for every contested call, even when the honest rationale is unglamorous: financial expedience won, and that's on the record rather than smoothed over.

### Where the real gains happen (Modeled → Integral)

The real shift is tying decision authority explicitly to budget authority, and requiring the prioritization framework itself to run on current inputs — this cycle's market intelligence (D1–D3), this cycle's requirements state (D4) — rather than a stale snapshot everyone politely pretends is still accurate. From there, the next gain is connecting prioritization to D8 (portfolio investment) and D11 (outcome signals) as live inputs, so AI can surface a recommendation and the PM function's job becomes ratifying or overriding it with documented reasoning, rather than assembling the case from scratch every cycle.

### What the top of the curve actually looks like (Telemetric)

At full maturity, every item in the portfolio carries documented tradeoff rationale tied to real market intelligence, real investment thresholds, and real outcome signals — continuously maintained, not reconstructed at review time. PM work shifts from arguing for priorities to ratifying AI-surfaced recommendations, reserving genuine PM judgment for the tradeoffs the model genuinely can't resolve on its own.

### Why this dimension matters

A backlog nobody can explain isn't just an optics problem — it's a signal that the organization has no reliable way to tell a good bet from a persuasive pitch. This dimension exists to make sure the order of the backlog is defensible evidence of investment logic, not a fossil record of whoever won the last argument.
